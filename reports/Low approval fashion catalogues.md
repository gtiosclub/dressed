# Find real fashion products without partnerships

**A club can test real fashion discovery without waiting for retailer approval, but it cannot obtain a verified, reusable catalogue of Depop, eBay, PacSun, and Hollister from one open source.** The fastest path is an on-demand shopping search API: SerpApi offers 250 searches per month for $0, and SearchApi offers 100 free requests. They return current search-result links and product fields, not a license to build a permanent database of retailer photos and offers. A small, retailer-named open dataset offers a second way to test imports, though its latest reported sync and image rights need checking. Dressed should keep user-shared links as its private Wishlist path and treat automated search as a separate, provisional Discovery feature. ([SerpApi pricing](https://serpapi.com/pricing); [SearchApi pricing](https://www.searchapi.io/pricing); [Livostyle dataset](https://github.com/arturayupov/womens-fashion-catalog-open-data); [Dressed architecture](https://github.com/gtiosclub/dressed/blob/main/docs/architecture.md))

## Two self-service APIs can show real search results quickly

**SerpApi is the best first live-search experiment.** Its Google Shopping endpoint returns a product title, merchant source, price, product link, and thumbnail. The free plan permits 250 successful searches per month and 50 per hour; the next plan is $25 per month for 1,000 searches. A single bounded backend request per user query could therefore support a small club demo without a retailer application. Coverage of any named shop, and whether a result remains available, must be measured rather than assumed. This is search retrieval, not a complete inventory of each merchant. ([Google Shopping API](https://serpapi.com/shopping-results); [pricing](https://serpapi.com/pricing))

**SearchApi is an alternative when eBay listings matter most.** It advertises 100 free requests without a credit card, then $40 per month for 10,000 searches, and documents an eBay search endpoint returning listing IDs, links, prices, and thumbnails. That endpoint extracts search pages; it is not eBay's official Browse API. SearchApi's terms put responsibility for rights to third-party text and images on the customer. Its legal protection does not cover the customer's use, storage, redistribution, or commercialization of those results. SerpApi likewise does not publicly grant a blanket right to cache and republish retailer product images in Dressed. For a v0, use a backend proxy, source attribution, outbound links, bounded query volume, and clearly provisional price and availability; resolve iOS display and image rights before public launch. ([SearchApi eBay endpoint](https://www.searchapi.io/docs/ebay-search-api); [SearchApi pricing](https://www.searchapi.io/pricing); [SearchApi terms](https://www.searchapi.io/legal/terms); [SerpApi legal](https://serpapi.com/legal))

**DataForSEO is more useful for batch evaluation than a live club demo.** Its Google Shopping API starts with $1 trial credit and requires at least a $50 top-up afterward. Its standard queue is priced at $0.001 per returned product, seller, or SERP and can take up to 45 minutes; the priority queue costs $0.002 with a stated one-minute target. Its legal FAQ excludes resale or redistribution of an untransformed feed or public raw-data endpoint without a separate agreement. These economics can be attractive for controlled experiments, but they do not solve catalogue rights or interactive latency by themselves. ([minimum payment](https://dataforseo.com/help-center/minimum-payment); [pricing](https://dataforseo.com/pricing/merchant/google-shopping-api); [legal FAQ](https://dataforseo.com/help-center/quick-answers-to-frequently-asked-legal-questions))

## One open fashion feed offers a narrow import test

The [Livostyle women's fashion dataset](https://github.com/arturayupov/womens-fashion-catalog-open-data) claims thousands of real products, product URLs, prices, variants, and thumbnails in JSON and Parquet. Its [MIT license](https://github.com/arturayupov/womens-fashion-catalog-open-data/blob/main/LICENSE) names Arcada LLC, which also appears on the retailer site, and permits reuse of the dataset with the license notice. That makes it a concrete **single-retailer importer test**, not a broad catalogue. The raw [statistics file](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/arturayupov/womens-fashion-catalog-open-data/main/data/stats.json) reports 2,938 products and a last sync of October 18, 2026, about 24 days before this review, despite the repository's weekly-sync claim. I could not independently verify that the repository publisher can sublicense supplier photographs or that a sample of its purchase links remains live. It is suitable for an internal parser and ranking trial after a link audit; public image display and current-price claims need further evidence. ([repository](https://github.com/arturayupov/womens-fashion-catalog-open-data); [Livostyle site](https://livostyle.com/); [statistics](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/arturayupov/womens-fashion-catalog-open-data/main/data/stats.json))

Open data does not usually supply a live offer. [Wikidata](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Licensing) is CC0 structured data, but not a current retailer price, stock, and purchase-link feed. [DummyJSON](https://dummyjson.com/docs/products) is explicitly sample ecommerce data; it is useful for repeatable UI tests, not real shopping. Public Shopify product JSON is also a technical endpoint rather than a general right to aggregate shops: Shopify describes its unauthenticated [Ajax API](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/ajax) as intended for Shopify-hosted themes. A willing small Shopify retailer could be a good one-shop collaboration, but the retailer's permission is the relevant access path. ([Shopify Storefront API](https://shopify.dev/docs/api/storefront/latest))

## Official broad marketplaces still require review

**eBay's official Browse API supports keyword and image search, but its sandbox has fictional listings and production access requires an eBay Partner Network application, review, and contracts.** Etsy's Seller App can be approved quickly for its owner's shop; a Personal App that reaches beyond one shop receives deeper review, with a further Commercial Access process for broader use. Amazon's Creators API requires an Associates account and at least ten qualifying sales in the prior 30 days. Best Buy has a self-service developer-key route and a live Products API, but its assortment is a weak test of trendy fashion and its content may be cached for at most 72 hours under its terms. These sources are useful for later integrations, not the fastest broad fashion v0. ([eBay requirements](https://developer.ebay.com/api-docs/buy/buy-requirements.html); [eBay sandbox](https://developer.ebay.com/api-docs/static/gs_understand-the-sandbox-and.html); [Etsy access](https://developers.etsy.com/documentation/); [Amazon Creators API](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/creatorsapi/docs/en-us/introduction); [Best Buy API terms](https://developer.bestbuy.com/legal))

For Depop and Vinted, no official approval-free, cross-seller catalogue feed was verified in this review. Dressed's existing reviewed URL import is the strongest low-friction way to let a user save an item from those services. It records a private, user-confirmed Wishlist link on-device; it does not verify the seller's price or availability or establish public catalogue rights. ([Depop Selling API](https://partnerapi.depop.com/api-docs/); [Vinted terms](https://www.vinted.com/terms-and-conditions); [Dressed product-link flow](https://github.com/gtiosclub/dressed/blob/main/dressed/Camera/ViewModels/ProductLinkStore.swift))

## Conclusion

**Build the v0 around two distinct experiences:** reviewed user-shared links for the user's Wishlist, and a small on-demand SerpApi trial for Discovery. This tests real shopping interest without blocking on a partnership or pretending that search results form a licensed permanent catalogue. In parallel, use a small Livostyle sample to exercise Dressed's existing importer after checking links and provenance. If the club later wants persistent product cards, photo indexing, or reliable live prices, choose a source whose terms and freshness contract actually support those features.
